# App Store Guideline 4.3(a) project audit

Audit date: 2026-09-27

This is a source-tree audit, not a guarantee of App Review acceptance. Apple can
compare account history, previously uploaded binaries, store metadata, signing
records, and assets that are not available in this repository.

## Executive summary

Troll Dash is not a thin content wrapper or a stock Flutter counter sample. It
contains a hand-written platformer loop, 29 named levels, multiple trap classes,
checkpoints, persistent progress, collectible coins, unlockable player skins,
sound effects, and custom-painted gameplay. The strongest evidence of a distinct
concept is the trap behavior: moving bait spikes, disappearing and fleeing
platforms, false goals, world swaps, reversed controls, darkness, ice, movement
echoes, and a second lap.

The audit did find residual scaffold material and inconsistent identifiers on
non-iOS targets. Those items have been removed or normalized. No obfuscation,
padding, hash-only changes, or review-detection workarounds were introduced.

## Findings and changes

### Product identity and UI

- The app consistently uses **Troll Dash**, the package name `troll_dash`, and
  the creator credit **Mias Ehrensperger**. The main menu previously presented
  only a conventional title plus play/shop actions, which could undersell the
  game's distinctive behavior in a short review session.
- The menu now previews three real mechanics—hidden hazards, world swaps, and
  second-lap traps—before the reviewer starts playing. This is a product-facing
  clarification of existing functionality, not artificial differentiation.
- The level selector is complete rather than empty: it exposes all 29 authored
  titles and gates them using persistent progress. The shop is functional and
  uses the game's coin economy; it is not a placeholder screen.
- The win and deliberate fake-win sequence are concept-specific. The fake win
  is part of the game's trap premise and leads to the remaining levels.
- The visual vocabulary is intentionally simple: Material controls plus
  geometric, code-painted platforms, spikes, flags, and a blue player. Although
  the implementation is custom, a static screenshot can therefore read like a
  generic platformer. The new mechanic preview helps, but a future art pass
  should only be undertaken with genuinely original direction and source files,
  not superficial reskinning for review.

### Flutter package and dependencies

- `pubspec.yaml` has a specific name and description. Its only runtime packages
  are `shared_preferences` (progress/economy persistence) and `audioplayers`
  (the seven game sound effects).
- The unused `cupertino_icons` dependency was removed. Stock instructional
  comments generated by `flutter create` were also removed from `pubspec.yaml`.
- `flutter_launcher_icons` remains a development-only dependency because it
  regenerates platform icon sets from the project's master artwork.
- There are no declared custom fonts, remote APIs, analytics SDKs, advertising
  SDKs, web views, URL launchers, or network-backed content.

### Assets and provenance

- Runtime media is limited to seven WAV effects under `assets/audio/`. The
  repository documentation records them as synthesized specifically for this
  project. Gameplay graphics are drawn by `CustomPainter`; there is no imported
  sprite pack or template art bundle.
- `assets/icons/app_icon.png` is the platform icon master, and the generated iOS
  `AppIcon.appiconset` supplies the expected sizes. The iOS launch images use
  matching game artwork on the game's sky color.
- The icon is coherent with gameplay, but its blue rounded character, sun,
  hills, and spikes are generic geometric motifs. Manually verify that the
  artwork was not derived from a tutorial/template and consider whether it is
  distinctive enough to identify Troll Dash without its title.
- The empty `assets/icons/.gitkeep` and Flutter's instructional
  `LaunchImage.imageset/README.md` were removed because they had no runtime use.
- Source inspection cannot prove external copyright ownership. Keep the source
  files or generation scripts, creation dates, licenses (if any), and layered
  originals available in case App Review asks for provenance.

### iOS review

- `Info.plist` declares **Troll Dash** / `TrollDash`, landscape-only operation,
  full-screen presentation, the standard Flutter scene delegate, and no usage
  descriptions or URL types. The scene configuration value `flutter` is an
  internal configuration name, **not** a registered URL scheme.
- All Debug, Profile, and Release iOS app configurations use
  `ch.gregiwaller.trollrunner`, matching the existing App Store distribution
  profile. Test identifiers use the same prefix. Other platforms retain their
  platform-specific `ch.gregiwaller.trolldash` identifiers.
- No iOS entitlements file is attached to the app target. The project requests
  no push, keychain group, associated-domain, iCloud, or Sign in with Apple
  capability.
- The app icon and branded launch art are configured in the asset catalog.
- No `DEVELOPMENT_TEAM` is committed, so there is no stale developer team ID in
  source control. Automatic signing must be assigned to the submitter's current
  team in Xcode or CI before archiving.
- The stock empty `testExample` native test was replaced by a product-identity
  test covering the display name and absence of URL schemes.
- The Xcode target/module name `Runner` and Flutter framework identifier
  `io.flutter.flutter.app` are normal Flutter build infrastructure; renaming
  them provides no genuine uniqueness and risks breaking tooling.

### Other platform metadata

- Stale `com.example` / `com.example.trollRun` identifiers and generic window
  titles were found in macOS, Linux, and Windows scaffold files. They do not set
  the iOS app identity, but were normalized to Troll Dash and
  `ch.gregiwaller.trolldash` so the repository has one coherent owner identity.
- Android already uses `ch.gregiwaller.trolldash` and the Troll Dash label.
  Its checked-in release configuration intentionally uses a debug key only for
  local runs; a private production signing configuration still must be supplied
  outside Git before distributing an Android build.

## Items to review manually in App Store Connect

1. Confirm the App Store Connect bundle ID exactly matches
   `ch.gregiwaller.trollrunner`, and that the uploaded archive is signed by the
   current, legitimate developer team.
2. Replace any generic or inherited app name, subtitle, promotional text,
   keywords, description, support URL, privacy URL, and marketing URL. Describe
   the authored reactive traps and 29-level progression accurately.
3. Use screenshots and an app preview captured from this exact build. Show the
   trap mechanic, level selector, world-swap/second-lap gameplay, checkpoint,
   and functional skin shop rather than only generic platforming imagery.
4. Check every localization, including inactive localizations, for old app or
   developer names. Also inspect review notes, in-app purchases, Game Center,
   custom product pages, and previous-version metadata.
5. Reply to App Review with a factual ownership/provenance explanation. If this
   source, icon, bundle ID, listing, or concept was ever acquired, transferred,
   white-labeled, licensed, or submitted from another account, disclose that
   relationship and provide documentation. Do not claim independence unless it
   can be substantiated.
6. Supply review instructions explaining landscape controls, how to reach the
   distinctive mechanics, and why the level-15 fake win intentionally continues
   into additional levels. Consider providing a clean review save state or
   precise steps, without hidden reviewer-only behavior.
7. Verify App Privacy answers against `shared_preferences` and `audioplayers`.
   This source audit found no analytics, ads, accounts, tracking, or network API,
   but the final binary and all integrated SDK privacy manifests must be checked.

## Items that cannot be changed automatically

- Apple's similarity signals, terminated-account linkage, prior binary history,
  signing history, and the exact apps Apple considers similar.
- App Store Connect metadata, screenshots, review correspondence, agreements,
  certificates, provisioning profiles, team membership, and account standing.
- Legal ownership and provenance of the concept, source, icon, and audio.
- Whether the bundle ID or assets were previously used outside this repository.
- Final signing configuration, because committing a personal team ID or profile
  would be unsafe and non-portable.

## Recommended submission evidence

Retain this audit, Git history showing incremental gameplay development, test
results, original icon/audio working files, and a short gameplay capture. In the
review response, focus on verifiable product history and concrete mechanics—not
on cosmetic changes or attempts to influence automated detection.
